# Research methods

Select methods by the claim being tested. Use only the structure that helps answer the question; report length and source count are not evidence standards.

## Match evidence to the question

- **Software behavior:** inspect official specifications and source at the relevant revision. Compare documented promises with implementation and, where authorized and practical, reproduce behavior with a minimal test. Record versions and environment.
- **Empirical effects:** examine study design, population, measurements, uncertainty, and confounders. Use systematic reviews for breadth and inspect underlying studies for consequential or disputed results. A review of unsuitable studies does not establish applicability.
- **Market or policy conditions:** use dated first-party records, statistics, regulations, or filings. Check geography, definitions, collection methods, and incentives. Distinguish announcements from observed outcomes.
- **Historical claims:** trace contemporaneous records and provenance. Distinguish the date of an event from later interpretation or republication.

Primary sources establish what their authors recorded, implemented, or claimed. They do not automatically establish independent accuracy. Judge authority, methods, incentives, currency, and applicability for each material claim; use secondary sources for context and synthesis with their evidentiary role explicit.

## Structure the investigation

- **Exploration:** identify actors, mechanisms, context, chronology, and open questions. Use reconnaissance to discover the boundaries before committing to a detailed outline.
- **Comparison:** define criteria from the user's decision before gathering evidence. Evaluate alternatives under comparable conditions and retain missing data rather than silently treating it as failure or success.
- **Causal explanation:** identify competing explanations and observations that would distinguish them. Seek counterexamples and alternative causes before recommending an intervention.
- **Forecast:** separate observed facts from assumptions. Identify scenarios, leading indicators, and conditions that would invalidate the forecast.

## Test a conclusion

Identify the assumption that would most change the answer if false. Seek evidence capable of rejecting it, preferably from an independent origin or a reproducible test. Use evidence not used to form the conclusion for a final check where available. If independent verification is unavailable, state that limitation.

Resolve apparent disagreement by checking dates, versions, populations, definitions, and methods. Preserve genuine disagreement rather than averaging incompatible findings or choosing a source by prestige alone.

## Bound the work

Set the effort bound in the investigation plan, such as bounded assignments and follow-up rounds. Stop when each decision-relevant question has adequate evidence or a documented limitation. Further sources earn inclusion when they address a gap, test independence, or could change the conclusion, not merely increase the bibliography.

An inconclusive result is valid when the report identifies what is known, why the remaining evidence is insufficient, and what observation would resolve the uncertainty.
